# The Man, the Girl

*By AA78. Writing sample provided by the author for voice reference. Untitled. Second-story sample, contemporary register.*

---

Night come on full. The humming of the streetlamps like the sound of something broken inside. The girl Ava out ahead. The man behind. Carrying between them only what they needed. Corn flakes and beer. Bread. They do not speak.

The street stretches away from them. Houses stand to witness. Once familiar. Now strange. As if the very street had slipped away and been replaced with something not quite true. The plastic of the shopping bag whispers with each step. A sound like something not yet dying.

The man. The girl. They walk. The plastic bag swings from his hand. A pendulum marking time.

Ava stops. Looks back at the man. Eyes dark pools in the sodium glare. What, the man says. She shakes her head. Resumes walking. Behind them the dollar store's sign flickers. Neon gone to seed. Ahead the skeletons of condominiums rise half born. Concrete and two-by-fours. A hive for other people's lives. The man follows. Each step an effort against the growing weight of night.

They pass a house. Now gleaming white. A porch where no one sits. A circular saw screams through the night. Ugly and cold. The man mutters. Fuck 'em, hope he breaks his neck. Voice low but Ava hears. Always hears. She turns. Fear grips her eyes but she does not speak it. The man looks away. Face twitches. Shame or anger. He does not know which. Does not want to know.

They reach their house. Concrete steps. The porch light buzzes. Insects live the night. The man fumbles for his key. Ava waits. Still. Patient. As if listening for something in the night. Something that has not yet spoken but will.

Inside. The door closes behind them. A barrier. But the man knows. Ava knows. There is no true barrier. It seeps in. Under. Through. In the air. And something else. Something that has always been there.

The man looks at his daughter. Sees the weight of knowing in her eyes. He turns away. Busies himself with the few bags. Drawing out the chore.

Ava watches. Silent. The man tries to stuff something down inside. Fails.

---

Morning come. Sirens come. The man wakes. Mouth dry. The man licks his lips. Copper. Head pounding. The man looks at the beer cans on the floor. Something in the air. A wrongness.

Ava already up. Sitting at the kitchen table. Bowl of corn flakes untouched. She looks at him. Says nothing. But her eyes. Her eyes speak plenty.

What's going on, the man says. Ava shrugs slow. Eyes low. Points to the window. He looks out. The block gathered in the street. Talking low. Gesturing at the house. The white house. The half flipped house.

The man dresses. Goes outside. Ava follows. Silent at his heels. They join the crowd. Whispers reach their ears. Accident, someone says. Bad fall, says another. Critical condition, comes a third voice. The man feels something cold settle in his gut. Remembering his words from last night.

A neighbor sees them. Nods at the man. Terrible thing, she says. Just terrible. Who'd have thought. A fall like that. Damn shame. The man nods. Cannot speak. Feels Ava's eyes on him. Heavy.

They return inside. The man pours cereal. Milk. Sits across from Ava. Turns the cereal in his mouth. Ava hasn't touched hers. Just watches him. Her eyes knowing. Accusing. Understanding.

You have to go to school, the man says. Ava nods. Gathers her things. At the door she pauses. Looks back at him. Dad, she says. Just that. Just Dad. Then she's gone.

---

School a thing unto itself. Ava walks the halls. Everything the same. Everything different. Voices buzz around her. Excited. Scared. Did you hear, they say. The accident. Maybe he won't make it.

Ava says nothing. Keeps her head down. But she listens. Always listens. Hears the fear. The excitement. Children are cruel. She feels it inside her. A churning. Something wanting to come out.

Class begins. Teacher talks. Words. Meaningless. All she can think about is her father's face this morning. The look. Guilt. Fear. Recognition. She'd seen it before. After mom died. When the bills piled up. When the neighborhood started to change.

A boy behind her whispers. Hey Ava. Heard your dad was there. Saw it happen. Is that true? She doesn't turn. Doesn't answer. The boy persists. Come on. Tell us. What'd he see? Did he look like this? Ava doesn't look. But knows the boy pulls a face. Hears the laughter. Far away and all around.

Shut up, she says. Voice low. Dangerous. The boy laughs. Pokes her with his pencil. What's wrong? Scared of a little blood?

She feels it first at the base of her spine. Something opens inside her. A burning behind her eyes. Awful through her veins. She turns. Looks at the boy. Hard. Sees the sneer on his face. And she wishes. Only for an instant.

The boy's face changes. Goes pale. His arms like lumber. Gasping. Eyes wide with terror. Blank and inhuman.

Ava blinks. Shakes her head. No! This isn't happening. Can't be happening. The boy makes a sound like breathing but not breathing. The teacher calls out.

Ava turns away. Faces front. She knows. Knows what her father knows.

---

The man sits at home. TV on but he doesn't see it. Doesn't hear it. Mind elsewhere. On falling bodies. On words. Anger. On something inside he never wanted, and still after all can not fully see.

Phone rings. He answers. Ava's school. She's gone, they say. Left in the middle of class. Haven't seen her since. The man feels fear grip his heart. Cold and tight.

He's out the door before he knows it. Into the streets. Calling her name. Ava. Ava. But streets don't answer. Only echo his words back at him. Accusing.

He searches. Hours pass. Sun high overhead now. Sweat on his brow. Fear inside. Where would she go, he thinks. Where would I go if I were her. If I had this.

Then he knows. The park. Where they used to go. Before. When they were just a family.

He finds her there. Sitting on a swing. Not swinging. Staring at nothing. He approaches. Slow. Careful. As if she might spook and take to run like a wild thing.

Ava, he says. She looks up. Eyes red. Face streaked with tears. Dad, she says. Her voice small. Broken. I did something. Something bad.

He sits on the swing next to her. Metal creaks under his weight. For a long moment, neither speaks. Then, What happened, he asks. Not wanting to know. Needing to know.

She tells him. About the boy. The sound, His eyes. The man listens. Heart broken and somehow still breaking. For her. For himself. For what they've become.

She finishes. Silence falls. Heavy and still. Finally, the man speaks. It's not your fault, he says. But the words ring hollow. Even to him.

Ava looks at him. Really looks at him. You knew, she says. A statement. You knew about this. About what I, about what I did.

The man nods. Can't lie to her. Not now. Not about this. How long, she asks. Since before you were born, he answers. Since always.

More silence. Then, What do we do now, Ava asks. The man has no answer. Only fear. Only the weight of a thing inside neither of them wanted. Neither of them can control.

We go home, he says finally. We go home and figure this out.

They stand. Start walking. Together.
