# The Darkling Beetles

*By AA78. Writing sample provided by the author for voice reference.*

---

The Darkling Beetles emerge each day at dawn. Their black bodies iridescent with cesium and smelling of orchids. You wake, their gentle feet padding upon your cheek. Your apartments deep in the womb of The Apostolic Palace of our Blessed Lady Death Eternal sighs. Morning prayers echo from bowed heads through the high vaults. Down the hall you hear the echo of bare feet on concrete. One hundred children dressed in saffron scrims shift cylinders of glass to switch the lights one by one from red of night to white of day. You know without being told that Maria the tallest of the girls was truly called by Lady Death and did follow the pillar of black light across the desert. Of the hundred she alone is blessed.

Sister Constanza enters without knocking. "Blessed morning, Your Holiness" she says, not meeting your eyes. No one meets your eyes, save the children. Three novitiates follow, carrying brass basins of purified water and steaming towels. You rise though you do not wish to, your slender toes flex against the concrete floor. You draw your long black hair across your shoulder. Peevishly the beetles pull apart their carapace. A sound like dry paper. The wings translucent and private as if embarrassed by your sight. They rise, begrudgingly. Bodies hanging in air like something destined for the dirt.

The novitiates approach, their hands both clasped and concealed within their ash gray sleeves. The bills of their habits bubble up and down as they waddle towards you. The oldest novitiate, Teresa, carefully collects the Darkling Beetles that have fallen. They will be preserved in resin and distributed to pilgrims who have traveled from the south. Each of the novitiates in turn reach out to unclasp one of the three buttons between your breasts. You raise your arms above your head. The youngest, Sarah, lifts away the night shirt. Folds it. Lips trembling in prayer. Hands calm and silent.

Your body belongs to the faithful.

Sister Constanza lights the censer, "Cum hoc, spiritum lavamus". The water is warm. The air is cool. Each part of the body is washed in order, like relics upon the altar. But special attention is paid to the left hand, where Cardinal Suárez cut your palm on your fifteenth birthday and pressed it to the black stone. The wound will never heal, until the world is healed. The sisters wring your bath water back into the brass basins. Water that washed your skin will wash away the kiss of death, and prevent stillbirth, or so it is said.

Father Dominguez waits outside your chamber. His radiation burns form a cross upon his ruined face, a miracle that secured his position as your guardian in the days of your girlhood. "The Archbishop requests you wear the Vestments of Sorrow today" he says. "It is the anniversary of the Boiling of the Pacific."

You exhale the thought. The Vestments of Sorrow weigh forty pounds, black wool interwoven with lead thread. The dress requires the three sisters to maneuver your body beneath it. You stand heavier now by a third. The bodice bows your hips, breathing laborious, your suffering a mirror to the world's. The lead a shield against the radiation as faith is our shield against death, or so it is said.

Breakfast is served in the Apostolic Refectory. The chamber stretches sharp and cold. The vaulted ceiling a gilded fresco painted with scenes of the irradiated rapture where flesh peels away in ribbons of light. Twelve acolytes, each ensconced in an archway all his own stand facing you. Shiny rubber gloves hold silvery forks with handles long as spears. You sit alone at the center of the long black table, hands folded in your lap. The Archbishop and three Cardinals observe from a high balcony, their faces hidden behind black masks adorned with silver and gold. Though you but glance across the chasm you can make out the shape of their machination. Not wicked, but human.

Sister Constanza approaches with a crystal bowl containing exactly three ounces of irradiated pomegranate seeds harvested from the trees in what was once Chapultepec Park. She cannot feed you directly, for fear your kiss of death will come to her too quick. Instead, two acolytes extend their forks, impossibly long and made of stainless steel. They work in perfect unison, spearing three seeds at a time, crossing the forks in the air before your mouth. You do not reach for the food; saints do not hunger. You must only open the mouth precisely when the forks arrive, accepting each morsel with humility.

The seeds contain enough radiation to burn a body from within, but not The Body. The Cardinals record this, yet another miracle in these the days of wonder, inside their leather-bound ledgers, noting how your throat glows faintly as you swallow.

"The Northern Heretics have sent another delegation," Father Dominguez says while you eat. "They claim to have recovered more Relics from before the war."

As always, you do not speak.

A brief pause.

"I shall inform them that you shall not meet them today".

A slight bow, and an aging man backs slowly down the long table.

At precisely nine, you are led to the Radiance Chamber where fifty-seven of the sick lie waiting. Bodies twisted, mutation, radiation. You will lay hands on each hairless head. Some will die, some will live. The ratio does not matter, only that you touched them, saw their suffering and blessed it.

A child among them stares up at you, unblinking, as a fly drinks a tear from her eye. She waits until your hand reaches her forehead before whispering, "Are you really a saint?"

The question freezes the air. Father Dominguez steps forward, ready to remove the child, but you raise your hand, he stops and the air moves once more.

You lean in close to her scorched ear and whisper, "What do you think?"

"I think you're just a woman," she says. "Like the before-time, beautiful and clean".

Something shifts inside you, too small to be felt, too large to be ignored.

You kiss her head. She smiles softly as the suffering of her short life subsides.

By noon, your arms ache. The stigmata on your palm begins to weep a substance that is not quite blood. It smells of ozone and copper. You hold out your arm and one of your attendants produces an apparatus perfectly designed for the collection of this fluid.

Lunchen is a public affair in the Great Hall. You sit alone at a table while two thousand faithful watch in silence as you consume exactly thirteen spoonfuls of engineered algae paste. You perform the Blessing of Sustenance, allowing the Archbishop to collect your saliva in a golden chalice. It will be diluted and rationed. You stand, the crowd does not, you walk away as they kneel.

In the afternoon, you are permitted thirty minutes of solitude in the Garden of Remembrance, a cavernous underground chamber where they've reconstructed what they believe parks looked like before the fires. The trees are hand carved. The closer leaves are made of silk, and the farther leaves hand-painted by artisans. You sit beneath the largest tree and turning your face to the incandescent sun you close your eyes.

When you open them, the leader of the Northern Heretics stands before you. She wears the uniform of an army which no longer exists. In her arm she holds the child from before now dressed in white.

"How did you get in here?" you ask, breaking your vow of silence.

"Your guards believe in miracles," she says. "They are easy to deceived." She places a metal box at your feet. "We've brought something from the Arctic Vault."

The box is small, made of tin. You turn it over in your hands somehow you know it contains a single seed. As you make to open it you are awakened by Sister Constanza.

The day goes. Your final duty awaits in the Chamber of Tears, where you're suspended by silver wires above the congregation. Your tears, induced by a herb they place beneath your eyelids, fall upon the faithful below. The Archbishop collects the final tear in a vial he wears around his neck. He clutches the it close and kisses it.

When finally they return you to your chamber, your hands rest on your abdomen. The beetles gather there, their wings suddenly vermilion and luminous. The sisters prepare you for sleep, applying ointments to your stigmata, combing your hair with ivory brushes, changing you into your night shirt, they notice nothing.

Tomorrow will be the same. Each day, the same. The ritual will keep what remains of the world.

Except for the secret growing within. Except for the child who will be born with a beetle's wings instead of arms, whose tears will truly heal, and whose coming was foreseen in the dreams of a billion dying children.
